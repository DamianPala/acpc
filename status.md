**Stage B is implemented on the daemon path.** A deferred continuation is registered in the daemon's in-memory turn table before reservation, adapter startup or restore; `status` overlays that table's `preparing` phase without writing a preparation marker. The normal turn claim is made on disk so a killed daemon is read as `orphaned`, while failed verification rolls back from an in-memory snapshot — and the reservation covers only that synchronous claim, so one session's slow restore no longer stalls the daemon's event loop. `stop` and Ctrl-C cancel preparation into a `cancelled` turn with a no-prompt placeholder, releasing the reservation and adapter binding; `steer` follows with its instruction plainly and says that nothing was interrupted. A cancelled restore exits `AcpcClient.replaying` through the existing `ReplayTracker.close` path: the generation is closed but retains any unaccounted tagged frames until their late callbacks are accounted, preserving the retention-over-forgetting asymmetry.

**The narrow third review caught the routing window being handed Ctrl-C's semantics wholesale.** Making SIGINT correct there installed a handler before the route was known, and that handler had to guess what SIGTERM meant: it guessed `terminated`, so a SIGTERM during routing exited 130 instead of 143, and one landing in the single event-loop iteration between routing completing and the handlers being corrected cancelled a turn the daemon had already accepted rather than detaching from it. Both faces were reproduced, the second by widening the gap deliberately rather than by racing it. The fix makes the guess unavailable: the handler records which signal arrived and nothing more, and detach-versus-terminate is resolved at the two points where the route is actually known. A classification that cannot be computed early cannot be computed wrong.

**The measurement engine was descoped after nine reviews, and the reason is a pattern rather than a defect.** It measured four probes per mode — an inside write, an outside write, a shell command, a network reach — and decided each verdict from what reached disk. Review 8 and review 9 both returned the same class: a fact derived from a different operation than the one that produced the effect. Round 12 moved that class downstream of the selector; round 13 collapsed causal membership to a single monotone rule at one code point; each redesign closed every instance anyone could name, and each was followed by the class reappearing somewhere the redesign had not modelled. Review 9's instance is the cleanest statement of why: membership is append-only while the marker predicate underneath it is not, so a marker going `absent → ok → wrong → ok` leaves the operation that produced the *first* `ok` permanently causal, and a later plain operation restoring the final state borrows its approval. Changing nothing but that operation's frame timing flipped the verdict between `escalated-allowed` and `allowed`. A monotone rule over a non-monotone predicate cannot be correct.

**Replay silence holds now, on both paths, because a frame's identity stopped being inferred.** Backlog item 14. Every previous attempt re-identified a replayed message *after* its provenance had been discarded — first by comparing serialized content, then by counting — and each one closed the symptom the last review had produced. The fix is to stop discarding it: the validated top-level `sessionId` is captured at the receive-to-dispatch seam, before ACP's router merges the peer's `_meta` over the params, and every replay generation tags its own frames with connection, session, generation and frame number. Suppression then matches a tag rather than a rendering, so the direct path's `"messageId": null` asymmetry — where the raw and parsed sides serialized differently and a protocol-valid frame slipped the filter into `answer.md` — has nothing left to exploit.

**The daemon hole was the wider one, and it is closed by the same fact.** `_MultiplexClient` resolved a callback by whatever was bound at callback time, so a late replay callback landed on a *live* client with an empty suppression register, after the restore client that held the record had been released in a `finally`. That is the path a warm `continue` takes, and it had no protection at all. The daemon now demultiplexes by the validated identity rather than by the `session_id` argument — which matters beyond replay: ACP may replace that argument from the peer's `_meta`, so a live frame whose top-level `sessionId` said `adapter-a` while its `_meta.session_id` said `adapter-b` was delivered to B, and B's transcript and answer got A's text. That was reproduced against a real `ClientSideConnection` and it was never replay-specific; round 1 implemented the rule for replayed frames only, which is why the review sent it back.

**On uncertainty the client retains rather than forgets, and that asymmetry is deliberate.** Retention costs memory and forgetting costs correctness, so a closed generation's records survive until closure is *proved*: `Connection.close()` sets its own `_closed` flag before awaiting its shutdown, so a cancelled or raising close leaves that flag set and a retry returns immediately having awaited nothing. Closure is therefore serialized on one shared task, shielded so a cancelled waiter cannot cancel the shutdown for everyone, with invalidation inside that task and only after the underlying close returns; a second caller waits on the same task instead of starting or skipping one. A bounded 64-generation cap evicts oldest-first with a diagnostic, so retention cannot grow without limit.

It was replaced rather than supplemented, by one that drives an *untagged live* frame through the real dispatcher and router, and confirmed red with the fix disabled. Every test added this round was checked the same way. A test whose name is accurate and whose mechanism is wrong is worse than a missing one.

**Landed 2026-08-12: a delivery record that cannot account for every prompt says so, instead of verifying against itself.** S3b's check compares a resume against the prompts acpc recorded as delivered, which is only worth anything if that record is complete. It could fall short two ways, and the quieter one was much the worse. A failed marker write loses an entry, and the check cannot see the loss: the replay then carries a prompt the record does not, and SPEC deliberately rules that extra replayed messages are not evidence of a mixup, so the comparison passes. But a connection offering no way to observe the outgoing prompt recorded *nothing* and raised nothing, and an empty list is a subsequence of any replay — so the comparison passed having compared zero prompts, and reported `verified`. That is the exact failure the honesty bullet exists to prevent, reached without any write ever failing.

Now acpc retries the marker write and, where a prompt crossed the wire but could not be recorded, marks the session's delivery record incomplete; a cold resume of such a session reports `resume: unverified — delivery record incomplete` in the summary and `--json` even where the subsequence check itself passed, and a resume that compared no prompts is never verified on the strength of that comparison. **The behaviour change worth naming: exhausted retries no longer fail the turn.** They used to raise, and by that point the prompt had already crossed the wire — so acpc failed a turn the adapter had received and was answering, discarding a real answer to protect a bookkeeping entry. The answer now survives alongside the flag.

**Two rounds of review earned their place, and the first finding is the one to remember.** Round 1 guarded a silently-failing write with another silently-failing write: `_mark_incomplete` suppressed `OSError` and `SessionError`, so with the marker writes failing *and* the flag write failing, a session finalized `done` carrying neither, and its next resume was falsely `verified`. The guard had the same defect as the thing it guarded, which is why it could not be fixed by adding another layer of the same kind — the flag now travels on the turn outcome and is written by the locked finalization that writes the answer and terminal state, so a successful `done` cannot omit it. Round 1's second defect was the inverse error: a prompt that failed *before* send was treated the same as an unobservable one, permanently poisoning a session whose record was in fact complete. The distinction the code was missing is between *unknowable* and *known not to have happened*, and only the first is incompleteness. The genuinely ambiguous fourth case — an observer installed, the prompt succeeding, and no frame seen — fails closed to incomplete, verified on both the direct and daemon seams.

Round 2's review found no behaviour defect at all and still returned do not ship, correctly: two daemon-only mutations left all 765 tests green, at exactly the propagation boundary where this codebase has previously re-derived a turn's fields instead of carrying them. Round 3 added those two regressions and changed no production code. Gates: 767 twice in parallel and once serially, ruff, format, pyright, smoke 581/581, and the incompleteness-dropping mutation re-confirmed red by hand before the commit.

**S3b landed 2026-08-11: a cold resume is verified before the new prompt is sent.** Reattaching by id was a claim, not a fact. Now, on every cold resume, the stored adapter session id must appear in `session/list` with a matching cwd, and where the restore replays the conversation the replayed *user* messages must contain the session's stored prompts as an ordered subsequence, compared as exact text because a prompt's own whitespace is part of it. Only prompts acpc recorded as having crossed the wire are compared, the two checks stand alone so an adapter that lists but never replays is verified by the listing alone, and a check that cannot run is reported rather than assumed: the summary and `--json` carry `resume: verified` or `resume: unverified — <what was unavailable>`. A mismatch fails the `continue` with an actionable error *before* the prompt is dispatched, so a failed verification costs nothing.

**Replay silence was not held when S3b landed, and had not been held before it either — closed by the entry above, kept here for the diagnosis.** SPEC's promise that replayed history changes nothing user-visible stood as the contract throughout; the implementation did not meet it, and that gap lived here and in the backlog rather than in the normative text. Two residues, both precise. On the direct path, late-dispatched replay callbacks are suppressed by comparing serialized content, and the two sides serialize differently — the raw side keeps `"messageId": null` while the parsed side drops it through `exclude_none=True` — so a protocol-valid frame slips the filter and replayed agent text reaches `answer.md`. On the daemon path there is no protection at all: the daemon binds a *restore* client, releases that binding in a `finally` (`daemon.py:705`), then binds a *new live* client under the same adapter session id (`daemon.py:836`), while `_MultiplexClient._for` resolves by whatever is bound at callback time (`daemon.py:97`) — so the suppression record lives on an object that has already been released, and a late replay callback lands on a live client with an empty register. That is the path `continue` uses under a warm daemon. Backlog item 14 (replay phase attribution) closed both.

The root of it, worth carrying forward: every round fixed the half that *collects* replay and none designed the half that *suppresses* it, so suppression kept being inferred from whatever symptom the last review produced — content key, then counting — each a different rendering of "re-identify a message after its provenance was discarded". The fix is to stop discarding it: attribute phase at receive time, per connection and adapter-session generation. A stopgap that normalizes both encoders was deliberately rejected: it would close the one reproduced direct-path instance while the daemon hole stayed wide open, and it would look like closure.

**Also deferred, with evidence, not guesses.** Three session-store defects the round-3 review found by fault injection are pre-existing rather than regressions, verified against `c3b7cb0`: `rotate_turn` already parked prompt and answer with `os.replace` before writing metadata with no rollback, and `_finalize` already suppressed only `SessionError` and never `OSError`; stale orphan detection sits in the same pre-existing liveness code. They share one root — session state is a set of independent file writes with no transaction, and liveness is inferred from data that can go stale between the read and the decision — so they go to 0.7 as a durability slice rather than being patched one at a time (backlog item 13). The reverse meta/transcript read window joins them: round 5 closed the dangerous direction, and what remains is a lock-free reader seeing the transcript ahead of `meta.json`, which is the safe direction and the same root. `daemon stop` acknowledging before teardown completes is item 12. A marker-persistence blind spot — a prompt observed crossing the wire but never persisted was forgotten, so later resumes reported `verified` against a record missing it — is resolved by the delivery-record incompleteness marker. The same marker covers connections that cannot observe outgoing prompts, and remains set for the lifetime of the session even when later turns record their prompts successfully.

**Incident record, corrected 2026-08-10 — there was no dispatch failure.** During Wave 2 six builder dispatches were cancelled across three rounds, and I diagnosed it in turn as an external `pgrep` sweep, a self-cancellation hazard, and a flaky dispatch channel. **S3a landed 2026-08-10: replay is consumed silently, and a cold resume prefers `session/resume`.** ACP requires an agent to replay the whole conversation during `session/load`, and acpc handled that replay two different ways. On the daemon path `mux.bind` ran *after* the load and the multiplexer drops updates for unbound sessions, so replay was discarded — the right outcome reached by accident. On the direct-spawn path the client is attached at spawn, *before* the load, so replayed agent messages flowed into `answer.md` and the transcript: a cold `continue` there prepended the entire previous conversation to the one file a caller reads for the answer. Both routes now go through an explicit replay guard on `AcpcClient`, so silence is a promise rather than a side effect, and the collected user messages are kept in a sink for the verification half to consume. A cold resume now prefers `session/resume` where the adapter advertises it, falling back to `session/load`; observable behavior is identical, and it is what keeps `continue` working when ACP v2 removes `session/load`.

The review was worth more than the implementation. It proved the guard airtight rather than arguing it: traced the ACP library's dispatch to show that one `asyncio.sleep(0)` really does reschedule the caller behind every already-queued notification task, then built a stress adapter replaying 400 chunks across many socket reads and measured zero leakage over ten runs, against 400 leaked chunks with the guard neutralised. It also found the one real weakness — `test_cold_load_resume_reapplies_model_and_effort` did not pin what it claimed. It exists because codex-acp#343 resets model and effort during `session/load` and acpc survives only by re-applying them *afterwards*; but the mock never modelled the reset, so hoisting `apply_call_options` above the restore kept the suite green while silently breaking cold resume against the real adapter. The mock now clears model and effort on load, faithful to the vendor bug, and the mutation confirms the test fails on the wrong ordering.

Also from review: the capability error now names both routes ("an adapter that can restore a session — ACP session/resume or session/load"), since accepting either made the old loadSession-only wording incomplete. Worth knowing for later: `session/resume` is registered as unstable in agent-client-protocol 0.11.1, so no adapter built on that library advertises it yet — the new branch is correct but dormant in the field.

## Decisions

- 2026-09-21 (1.0 closing, slice 23): **A cancel that lands before any prompt keeps its own stop reason, `canceled during preparation`, in the public spelling.** SPEC's new "one spelling of a canceled turn" sentence would have folded it into `canceled`, but the distinction is observable and load-bearing: the answer file is a placeholder, and `steer --steer-mode cancel-then-start` reports "nothing was interrupted" off it. Both historical spellings are normalized on read; the ACP wire literal `cancelled` stays in protocol objects only.
- 2026-09-21 (1.0 closing): **The branch is merged as-is, version 1.0.0, no squash.** The refactor of `cli.py` stayed at consolidation (helpers, two function splits) rather than a package split, because the structure recon found duplication, not architecture; the version jump records the surface break MIGRATION.md describes (`status`→`list`, `rm`→`delete`, `stop`→`cancel`, `-o`→`--output-file`, tagged text off a terminal, exit 124 without a result).
- 2026-09-20 (phase 0.2, slices 16-19): **A client deadline returns no result and creates no file; `wait` reports the turn it started on.** Before, `--timeout` printed a partial document and could describe a newer turn than the one the caller watched. Both are false reports; the answer so far is in the session files and `log --tail` reads it.
- 2026-09-20 (slice 17): **A usage limit is waited out inside the same acpc turn.** ACP has no continuation, so the resumption is a second `session/prompt` on the same adapter session; the turn number, answer file and token count stay one turn's, and `cancel` in `waiting` drops the resumption without contacting the adapter.
- 2026-09-20 (slice 18): **Off a terminal, `text` is a tagged document; the answer is never escaped, only bounded by counted tags.** Escaping the six wrapper tags inside the answer (draft.9) altered the agent's words; draft.10's `<answer-N>` keeps them verbatim and lets a reader validate the boundary by line count. `next` is always the last metadata key so it lands after `truncated`/`output_file`.
- 2026-09-20 (slice 19): **`continue` with no message is a resumption, not an empty turn.** Accepted after canceled/failed/unknown with acpc's fixed instruction; a usage error after succeeded; the active-session conflict is checked before the message, so a running session never gets `invalid_input`. `pending_corrections` is always `null` because acpc cannot see the adapter's queue and must not report zero.
- 2026-09-14 (slice 13, Damian): **`steer` defaults to in-place where the adapter supports it; cancel-then-start stays as the explicit second mode.** The 2026-08-06 decision that steer is interrupt-based rested on ACP having no mid-turn injection; both installed adapters now ship `_session/steering`, measured live. Mode names and `correction_result` follow the standard's `conversational` vocabulary without declaring the extension, so a later declaration is not a rename. `--cancel-after` requires an explicit `--steer-mode cancel-then-start`, a static rule rather than one that depends on runtime capability. An adapter that starts its own turn (`startedNewTurn`) is cancelled and reported `outcome_unknown`; no outcome ever falls back to cancel-then-start automatically.
- 2026-08-12 (0.6): **Stage B promises a settled state, not the same state.** The bullet first promised that a `continue` following a cancelled restore "starts from the same state the cancelled one did". Round 1 proved that undeliverable: ACP defines no cancellation for `session/load` or `session/resume`, so the restore completes adapter-side after the cancel and mutates the session, and the only mechanism that reliably prevents it is closing the ACP connection — which destroys the warm adapter and every other session on it. The promise now covers what acpc actually controls, what a turn is allowed to run against, and the limit it does not control is stated in the same bullet rather than omitted. Keeping the old wording would have made the SPEC assert something the tool does not do; nobody later reads the weaker promise as a quiet retreat if the retreat is written down.
- 2026-08-12 (0.6): **`probe`'s measurement engine descopes to 0.7 under a rule armed in advance.** Any further instance of the derive-a-fact-from-the-wrong-operation class ended the slice, and review 9 returned one: causal membership is append-only while the marker predicate beneath it is not, so a verdict could change with nothing but a frame's arrival order. Two architectural attempts is the budget. `probe --discover` ships alone, and a bare `probe` is a usage error rather than a silent answer to the cheaper question.
- 2026-08-12 (0.6): **A signal handler records which signal arrived; what it means is resolved where the route is known.** The routing window had been given Ctrl-C's semantics wholesale, so SIGTERM there exited 130 and one arriving as the route resolved killed a daemon-owned turn instead of detaching. The fix is structural rather than careful: the handler is no longer given the route, so it can no longer guess it.
- 2026-08-12 (0.7 scope, Damian): **network access becomes a measured mode fact.** What a mode permits on the network is recorded alongside the filesystem facts, and the probe engine's rework accounts for it from the start rather than gaining it later — the same vocabulary change backlog 16 already needs for `requires` and loud divergence.

- 2026-08-08: **Permissions and modes collapse into one scale, and acpc always sets the mode.** Leaving `session/set_mode` unsent hands the policy to the vendor's config: measured, `acpc run claude --permissions all` produced a callee that could do nothing, because the vendor home pins `defaultMode: "dontAsk"`. The highest policy acpc offers was inert and said so nowhere.
- 2026-08-08: **A mode is described by two facts, not one.** Sol's review killed the single-scalar version and the probe agrees: `acceptEdits` auto-allows edits *and* shell writes while still delegating the destructive call, and claude's `default` runs commands its own classifier deems read-only without emitting a request. `grants` bounds what happens unasked; `delegates` says whether anything arrives. Only `grants` is a bound acpc can state, so `edit` promises what a callee may change, never that no shell ran.
- 2026-08-08: **`switch_mode` is a re-selection, not a permission kind.** The first draft classified it as read, which would have let a callee under `--permissions read` ask to enter `bypassPermissions` and be allowed — reinstating the bypass the redesign deletes, and `plan` requests a switch in normal use. The target now goes through the same ceiling as the initial selection; a refusal ends the turn naming the mode and the policy that would admit it, so the caller raises the ceiling and resumes with `continue` (variant A, chosen over parking the turn on a reverse IPC channel).
- 2026-08-08: **The `delete` rung is dropped and `other` stays unknown.** Neither claude nor codex ever emitted `delete` or `move` — a deletion arrives as `execute`, because the callee shells out. Across 48 sessions and 266 permission requests on this machine the only kinds seen were `execute`, `edit`, `switch_mode` and `fetch`. Review feedback had argued `other` was common enough that denying it made behaviour arbitrary; on this evidence it does not occur, so the `execute` rung is not widened to admit it.
